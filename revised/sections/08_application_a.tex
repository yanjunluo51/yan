\section{Illustrative numerical application to a 600-MW CCUS retrofit}

A 600-MW coal-fired CCUS retrofit supplies the inputs of a specific project for this illustration. The application combines the economic calibration of this retrofit with an ambiguity radius from a date-reconstructed policy-text signal. A four-member reference profile of recorded weights and the archived price series complete its four inputs. From these inputs, the method returns member thresholds and quarterly contested-price intervals, against which each archived daily China Emission Allowance (CEA) price is classified. The classification is retrospective, because the anchor, corpus, and encoder are fixed ex post. For example, the volatility anchor estimated on 2024 and 2025 data applies to the entire historical signal path. Sensitivity checks varied the weight profiles, economic inputs, volatility anchors, and scaling constants.

\subsection{Numerical setting and policy-text signal}

\subsubsection{Corpus and signal construction}

The corpus comprises 682 central-government energy and climate policy records from four issuing bodies. These bodies are the National Development and Reform Commission, the National Energy Administration, the State Council, and the Ministry of Ecology and Environment. Of these records, 678 fell inside the analysis window from 2016Q1 to 2025Q4. The records were retrieved from the PKULaw database, which assigns each one a stable document number.

The retrieval targeted the years 2016 to 2025. A thematic screen based on source tags and a title keyword filter retained records classified under energy, climate, carbon markets, or emissions trading. A document-type screen kept substantive plans, notices, guidance, and formal responses while excluding press releases, routine administrative notices without policy content, and non-Chinese texts.

The policy-text signal is quarterly, so an incorrect document date can place a record in the wrong quarter and distort the signal path. Dates in the database may differ from official issuance dates. An audit therefore matched each record to an official agency page where possible and located such a page for 414 of the 682 records. Of the two date assignments compared, the reference assignment uses the date printed in each archived record and is reproducible from the archive alone. The page-date assignment uses a verified agency page date where available and the printed date otherwise. The two assignments produced different paths for the quarterly signal and the interval width. Window membership also follows the printed dates: four records bear 2015 dates and fall outside the window, so they are excluded. Four other records carry month-only dates inside the window and are retained, because a month identifies the quarter.

Each document is represented by precomputed layer-14 embeddings from the Qwen3-0.6B model \citep{Yang2025}. The embeddings are pooled across subword tokens with term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) weights and normalized to unit length. This primary representation, Qwen3-L14, was fixed before the signal was compared with realized volatility. From these unit vectors, the policy-semantic-dispersion index is
\begin{equation}
\PSDI_t=1-\lVert\bar{c}_t\rVert,
\label{eq:psdi}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{c}_t$ is the centroid of all normalized document vectors in period $t$. A larger $\PSDI_t$ indicates greater dispersion around the period centroid. The rolling variability signal tracks the temporal variation of this dispersion through the four-quarter standard deviation
\begin{equation}
w_t=\mathrm{std}_4(\PSDI_t,\PSDI_{t-1},\PSDI_{t-2},\PSDI_{t-3}).
\label{eq:wt}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm{std}_4$ is the population standard deviation, with divisor four, over the current and previous three quarters. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} abbreviates this window as $\PSDI_{t:t-3}$.

Replacing the dispersion index by six alternative statistics on the same vectors moved the peak quarter in two cases, although none changed the gate outcome. One corpus-composition check moved both the peak quarter and the peak width. This check excluded documents longer than 2,000 characters before truncation and retained 384 of the 678 records. The peak quarter shifted from 2024Q4 to 2023Q1, and the peak interval width rose from 6.63 to 8.59\,CNY/t. The Spearman rank correlation between this length-filtered path and the reference path was 0.61. One of two replacement encoders, MacBERT-base, also moved the peak, placing it in 2019Q2 at a width of 5.42\,CNY/t. Its quarterly signal had a Spearman rank correlation of 0.305 with the reference path.

\subsubsection{Screening procedure and input diagnostics}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate} summarizes the gate update from the registered radius source through the release rule to the reported object. Its computational cost depends on the radius source, and with a declared radius the affine branch is $O(m)$ in the panel size $m$. With the text-derived radius, it is $O(\lvert D_t\rvert+m)$ once the embeddings exist, where $\lvert D_t\rvert$ is the number of documents in period $t$. This bound assumes a fixed embedding dimension and a four-quarter history. The fallback adds one benchmark optimization per member. Computing the release envelope of one calibration-radius cell on the 2,001-point weight grid took about 0.2 seconds, a wall-clock figure varying with machine load.

In the text branch of Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate}, the proportional map $h_t=c_w w_t$ requires a nonnegative signal, since $h_t$ is a radius. The rolling standard deviation is nonnegative by construction. The reference configuration sets the scaling constant $c_w$ to one, so the ambiguity radius equals the signal before the admissibility cap. An estimated $c_w$ would need a stable association between the lagged signal and realized volatility. Before the national emissions trading scheme (ETS), this association was positive but imprecise. Over the 18 quarters of the national ETS period, the estimated slope turned negative at $-18.07$, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[-34.57,-1.57]$. The confidence interval for the shorter estimation window spanned zero. Neither subsample identified a stable value for $c_w$. Five further diagnostics, covering directional association, temporal-order permutation, construct validity, an event-style check, and national ETS stability, supported no stable mapping either. The signal therefore serves as a scenario input configuring the radius.

Slope estimates distinguishable from zero, such as the national ETS slope, still left the scale of the radius open. Propagating the pre-ETS slope to the thresholds gave a width envelope of up to 19.2\,CNY/t. This value was about 2.9 times the configured peak width of 6.63\,CNY/t. The pre-ETS slope and the national ETS slope differed by 61.08, with a standard error of 30.94. The 95\% confidence interval for this difference, based on the $t$ distribution with 23 Welch degrees of freedom, contained zero. With slopes of opposite sign and a difference indistinguishable from zero, the data did not fix even the direction of a rescaling. A mapping re-estimated on the national ETS window explained none of the out-of-sample variation in 2024 and 2025. Separately, the signal was compared with an established policy-uncertainty measure, the log China Economic Policy Uncertainty (EPU) index of \citet{Baker2016}. Over 2017 to 2024, the Pearson correlation was 0.265 ($n=32$), with a four-lag heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) $p$-value of 0.036. Over the 14 national ETS quarters covered by the EPU sample, it was 0.264.

Although the slope estimates left the radius scale open, two properties suit the signal to its illustrative role. First, an institution can archive and re-encode the documents. With the corpus, encoding pipeline, and scaling convention recorded, a past radius can be recomputed without re-eliciting any expert input. Second, the documents come from the same regulatory process whose ambiguity the committee evaluates at each gate.

Two calibration routes could replace the scaling convention with an estimate. A forward-looking route would set the endpoints from option-implied volatility bounds. This route requires a traded allowance option, and the national market had none over the sample period. A survey route would use the forecast dispersion elicited from allowance-price forecasters with a documented track record. Each source would need its own validation and an explicit mapping to the declared radius. Combining the two would need an aggregation rule for their overlapping information.

For prospective use, an institution should set a calibration cutoff and use only information available by this date. Before later gate outcomes are observed, it should also register a target radius $h_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and a strictly positive reference signal $w_{\mathrm{ref}}$, which fix $c_w=h_{\mathrm{ref}}/w_{\mathrm{ref}}$. The registration also covers the volatility-anchor window, the encoder version, the admissibility cap, and the review cadence. Later gate outcomes do not retune $c_w$ or the reference statistic. A documented drift or regime review opens a new dated configuration under the append-only rule. The target radius, achieved radius, rounded display value, and canonical registry key are distinct fields, because release matching follows the exact-key rule. Under this rule, an unseen radius sends the gate to benchmark recomputation. Prospective affine-only use therefore requires a radius discretization declared in advance or a release envelope certified over a continuous radius interval. A stable forward-volatility series may later support statistical calibration. The institution should then prespecify training and holdout windows and register the fitted map as a new configuration.

\begin{algorithm*}[!t]
\caption{Gate-level member thresholds and safeguarded classification.}
\label{alg:gate}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require One registered radius source, either a declared radius $h_t$ (or $\rho_t$) or the application-specific text construction with its registered pipeline identifiers
\Require Dated deployment record for $\sbar$, $c_w$, $\epsilon$, the registered price-recording buffer $b$, and registry-versioning rules
\Require Recorded weights $\{\alpha_i\}_{i=1}^{m}\in[0,1]^{m}$ passing the prespecified input-acceptance checks \Comment{reporting inputs}
\Require $r>\mu_c>0$, $I,Q>0$, prevailing price $P_t$, and reference project value $V_0$ inside the benchmark domain
\Require Versioned finite registry snapshot $\mathcal{R}^{(\nu)}$ with frozen canonical serialized configuration identifiers
\Require Direct-benchmark request flag $b_{\mathrm{direct}}\in\{0,1\}$
\Ensure Affine member table, $\tilde{\alpha}$, $C_t$, $B_t$, released classification, audit record, and reporting mode
\Ensure Benchmark table on fallback or direct request
\State initialize $\eR$, $\ER$, both proximity flags, and both certification flags to \emph{not evaluated} \Comment{filled when the lookup or check runs}
\If{the deployment record, economic, or recorded-weight checks fail}
  \State \Return input review required; issue no threshold report
\EndIf
\If{the registered radius source is a declared radius}
  \State read the registered $h_t$ or $\rho_t$
\Else
  \State obtain $h_t$ by the application-specific text construction; Eqs.~(\ref{eq:psdi}) and (\ref{eq:wt}) give $\PSDI_t$ and $w_t$, and the admissibility cap gives $h_t$ \Comment{application-specific step}
\EndIf
\State \textbf{Analytical layer.} $\rho_t\gets h_t/\sbar$; $(\sigma_{-},\sigma_0,\sigma_{+})\gets(\sbar-h_t,\sbar,\sbar+h_t)$
\State solve Eq.~(\ref{eq:char}) at $(\sigma_{-},\sigma_0,\sigma_{+})$ and compute $(\Vlow,\Vzero,\Vhigh)$
\If{$V_0\ge\Vlow$}
  \State \Return input review required; issue no threshold report \Comment{benchmark-domain check}
\EndIf
\State $V^{*}_{i}\gets\alpha_i\Vlow+(1-\alpha_i)\Vhigh$ for all $i$
\State $\tilde{\alpha}\gets1/2$ if $h_t=0$; otherwise $\tilde{\alpha}\gets(\Vhigh-\Vzero)/(\Vhigh-\Vlow)$
\State \textbf{Interface layer.} $P^{*}_{0}\gets\Vzero(r-\mu_c)/Q$; $P^{*}_{i}\gets V^{*}_{i}(r-\mu_c)/Q$ for all $i$; $C_t\gets[\min_iP^{*}_{i},\max_iP^{*}_{i})$; $B_t\gets\max_iP^{*}_{i}-\min_iP^{*}_{i}$
\State affine summary $\gets\textsc{classify}(P_t,\{P^{*}_{i}\})$ \Comment{three-state gate summary}
\State $\dedge\gets\min\{\lvert P_t-\min_iP^{*}_{i}\rvert,\lvert P_t-\max_iP^{*}_{i}\rvert\}$; $\dmember\gets\min_i\lvert P_t-P^{*}_{i}\rvert$
\State buffer-flag $\gets[\min\{\dedge,\dmember\}<b]$ \Comment{price-input diagnostic reported beside the classification}
\State $(k_{\theta},k_{\rho},k_{V})\gets\textsc{canonical\_ids}(r,\mu_c,\sbar,\rho_t,V_0/I)$
\State registered $\gets\textsc{registry\_lookup}(k_{\theta},k_{\rho},k_{V};\mathcal{R}^{(\nu)})$ \Comment{exact values; no rounded match or interpolation}
\If{registered}
  \State read the dimensionless registered envelope $\eR$, the grid maximum recorded for the cell \Comment{$I$ and $Q$ enter only through $P^{*}_{0}$}
  \State $\ER\gets P^{*}_{0}[\eR+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}]$; near-edge $\gets[\dedge\le\ER]$; near-member $\gets[\dmember\le\ER]$
  \State fallback $\gets b_{\mathrm{direct}}\lor[V_0/I\neq1]\lor[\rho_t>0.10]\lor$ near-edge $\lor$ near-member
\Else
  \State both proximity flags $\gets$ not evaluated; fallback $\gets$ true
\EndIf
\If{fallback}
  \State global-certified $\gets$ Condition~(\ref{eq:unique}) holds at $(\theta,\rho_t)$
  \If{global-certified}
    \State solve Eq.~(\ref{eq:Jalpha}) on $[\Vlow,\Vhigh]$ at $(\alpha_i,V_0)$ for all $i$
  \Else
    \State isolate every stationary point of Eq.~(\ref{eq:Jalpha}) on $[\Vlow,\Vhigh]$ and compare the objective at all of them and at both endpoints
    \If{no global maximizer is certified}
      \State \Return benchmark-model review required; issue no released classification
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \State one-sided-certified $\gets$ Condition~(\ref{eq:onesided}) holds at $(\theta,\rho_t)$ \Comment{governs the one-sided reading only}
  \State convert the benchmark triggers by Eq.~(\ref{eq:price})
  \State mode $\gets$ dual report
  \If{the affine and benchmark three-state summaries agree}
    \State released classification $\gets$ this summary \Comment{agreement concerns the summary; member assignments may differ, and both member tables are released}
  \Else
    \State released classification $\gets$ none; benchmark-model review required \Comment{no priority for either rule}
  \EndIf
\Else
  \State released classification $\gets$ affine summary; mode $\gets$ affine-only report
\EndIf
\State record the audit fields $I/Q$, $P^{*}_{0}$, $\eR$, and $\ER$, the radius source, the two certification flags, and the mode
\State \Return affine member table, $C_t$, $B_t$, $\tilde{\alpha}$, released classification, $(\nu,k_{\theta},k_{\rho},k_{V})$, the audit record, and the benchmark table on fallback or direct request
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm*}
